The 60 seconds

How to make it look real

  1. Generate products from stills, never from text alone. First make one reference image of each product below, ideally by photographing the real KAPTAN piece. Then use image-to-video for every shot, with that still as the first frame or reference. This is what keeps a bag from changing design between cuts.
  2. Keep logos out of the generation. Video models garble lettering. Every prompt asks for no text or logos. The K deboss, brass tags, luggage tag, woven label and belt box are added in post with a planar track (Mocha, After Effects or Resolve Fusion), using the real artwork at the sizes listed per product.
  3. Shoot the five branding inserts for real if at all possible. Shots 1, 6, 12, 21 and 22 are macro product moments. A half-day tabletop shoot with the actual products will beat any model at these, and they are the shots that sell craftsmanship.
  4. Lock people with a character reference. Generate one portrait per cast member and reuse it as a subject reference (Kling Elements, Runway References, Veo ingredients) in every shot they appear.
  5. Generate long, cut short. Generate 5–8 s per shot and use the calmest 1.2–4.5 s. Reject any take with warped straps, extra handles, unnatural hands or a bag that floats.

Grade warm but not orange: lifted blacks around 3%, highlights rolled off, skin kept true. Light is mostly motivated by windows and late sun, with one soft key per shot. Keep the lens language consistent: macro inserts at 90–100 mm, people at 35–65 mm, wides at 24 mm. Nothing faster than a slow push or a walking-pace track.

The wordmark is set in ivory rather than the logo's yellow, so the yellow is saved for the final emblem. The alternate tagline “Elegance You Carry.
